The one number that matters non-users exposed → opted in → became active users

Every meeting Araka books also messages the counterparty — a person who's never used Araka. That's the entire growth thesis: a scheduling utility with a built-in, WhatsApp-native referral loop that Poke's single-player design structurally can't copy. This plan exists to find out if that conversion rate is real. Everything below is instrumented against it, not against raw sign-ups.

Who this month is for

Narrow on purpose. Chase people who already feel "did they confirm? will they show up?" pain and live in WhatsApp — not a general audience.

week 01 Aug 19 – Aug 25

Foundation & seed

Get instrumentation and message quality right before spending any distribution effort on it.

  • Instrument the loop — track three counts weekly: bilateral messages sent to non-users, opt-ins, and meetings booked by that new user within 14 days. A spreadsheet against users.consent_status and tasks is enough; no dashboard needed yet.
  • Direct outreach — 30–40 personal-network messages using the outreach template below. Target: people who already fit the ICP, not a cold list.
  • Community groundwork — join 4–5 India-focused WhatsApp/Telegram/Discord communities (indie hacker, freelancer, small-broker groups). Lurk and add value this week — no pitching yet.
  • Shoot the demo — a 15–20s screen recording of the actual bilateral flow: booking a meeting, the confirm tap, the message landing on the other person's phone. This is the single asset every later channel reuses.

Content & community push

Put the demo in front of people who'd feel the pain, without looking like an ad.

  • Organic posts — the demo clip on X, LinkedIn, and Instagram Reels, framed as "built this because I kept losing time chasing confirmations," not a product launch.
  • Community posts — share in the groups joined last week, value-first: the problem it solves, offer to set people up personally, ask for blunt feedback.
  • Product Hunt / Peerlist prep — draft the listing (tagline, gallery, first-comment story) for a Week 3 launch day. Don't launch yet — a rushed listing underperforms a prepared one.
  • Keep direct outreach running — another 20–30 messages, now including anyone who liked/replied to the organic posts.

Paid test & launch day

Spend the entire test budget on one channel, not several — $100 split three ways teaches you nothing.

  • Product Hunt / Peerlist launch day — call in the personal network from Week 1 for early votes/comments; this is a distribution event, not a metric to optimize for its own sake.
  • One paid test — either a boosted Instagram/Meta post targeting India, 22–40, interested in productivity/small business tools, or a single nano-influencer (5–20k followers, productivity/hustle-culture niche) posting the demo. Pick whichever is cheaper to arrange this week; don't run both.
  • Double down mid-week — by Wednesday, look at which of Weeks 1–2's channels produced the best opt-in→active conversion, and shift the remaining organic effort there.

Read the loop, decide the next 30

Turn four weeks of data into one decision, made explicitly rather than by default.

  • Close the funnel numbers — outreach sent, opt-in rate, active users, and — the number that actually matters — non-user→user conversion off the bilateral message.
  • Kill what didn't work — cut channels that produced sign-ups but not repeat bookings; a booked-once user isn't validation.
  • Collect 2–3 real quotes — from users who had Araka handle a real reschedule or no-show save. These become the next round's proof, not the demo clip.
  • Hit the Day-30 checkpoint below before committing to another 30-day cycle on the same track.

What not to do this month

The strategy doc flags Meta's tolerance for a narrow WhatsApp bot as the single existential risk. Marketing is exactly where that gets tested first.

Positioning

Never market Araka as a "general AI chatbot" or "AI assistant" in the open-ended sense — every post, bio, and listing says scheduling. That framing is why Meta tolerates it on WhatsApp at all.

Consent

Growth comes from people messaging the bot themselves after seeing a post or hearing from you directly — never from importing or bulk-messaging a contact list to manufacture bilateral exposure.

Opt-out

Watch the STOP rate on bilateral messages weekly, not just at the Day-30 review. A rising opt-out rate is the earliest signal something in the copy or targeting is off.

Communities

Most India tech/founder groups ban self-promo without context. Lead with the problem and an offer to help, not a link — Week 1's "no pitching yet" groundwork is what earns the right to post in Week 2.

Day-30 checkpoint

This cycle runs consumer + bilateral loop first, by your call. Worth saying plainly: the strategy doc's own verdict is that SMB/concierge outreach — clinics, salons, tuition centers, brokers — is where durable ARR actually lives, because no-shows are a budgeted cost for them, not a nice-to-have.

So the decision at Week 4 isn't "did we get users" — it's whether the bilateral loop shows real virality: people converting because Araka messaged them, not because you personally reached out. If the non-user→user conversion rate holds up without your direct hustle behind it, keep pushing consumer. If it's flat once you stop manually driving it, redirect the next 30 days and the next test budget toward the 5–10 SMB pilot in araka-vs-poke-strategy.md instead.

Either outcome is useful data — the point of this cycle is finding out which one you're in.
